# Analysts

**What it is.** The analysts' mean 12-month target price, the range of their targets, the overall opinion and the number of analysts.

**The idea.** Analysts know the industry but are systematically optimistic and follow the price: after a rise they raise the targets, after a fall they lower them. This is one more source, not a forecast; your journal measures sources by their results.

**How to use it.**

- **Mean target** — compare with the price: +10–20% — the usual expectation; over +40% — strong belief or stale targets.
- **Target range** — from the lowest to the highest. Narrow — agreement; wide (2 times and more) — uncertainty: the outcome depends on who turns out right.
- **Opinion** — «buy» for most shares: selling is rarely advised. A change of opinion is worth more than the opinion itself.
- **Analysts** — under 5 — the numbers are unreliable; 20 and more — everyone studies the share, and its price already holds their view.

**When it matters.** As a check of your own thesis: if you expect a rise and the target is below the price — find what you are not seeing. To test the analysts — record their target as a «Target price» hypothesis and look a year later.

**Abbreviations.** PT — Price Target; PT Low–High — the targets' range; Consensus Rating — the average view (Strong Buy / Buy / Hold / Underperform / Sell); # Analysts — how many analysts.

**Reminder.** An opinion is not a reason to buy.

**Trap.** Buying because the mean target is 30% above the price: targets chase the price, not the other way round.
