# Fund ratios

**What it is.** For exchange funds: the size of the assets and the return year to date and over 3 years as a yearly rate. The fund's composition is in the block «Fund composition».

**The idea.** A fund has no earnings of its own: its valuation is that of the securities inside. What matters is the size (reliability and liquidity), the composition, and whether the fund does what it promises.

**How to use it.**

- **Assets** — over $1B — a reliable and liquid fund; under $50–100M — it may be closed, and the spread between buying and selling is wider.
- **Year to date** and **3 years, a year** — compare with the index of its market and with similar funds in «Compare with»: a fund behind its index for years — you pay for nothing.
- **The fund's fee (ER)** — in the line under the name, next to the size: what the fund takes a year of what is invested. 0.03–0.2% — cheap (large index funds), 0.5–0.9% — usual for country and sector funds, over 1% — dear. The fee comes off the price unseen, every day: over 10 years 0.6% a year eats about 6% of what was invested. To compare the fees of similar funds — the «Countries» screen and «Compare with».
- Look into the **Fund composition**: which sectors and countries are inside — these go into the portfolio's allocation and the job's risk.

**When it matters.** When choosing between similar funds and when reviewing the portfolio: two funds with the same composition do not make the portfolio more diverse.

**Abbreviations.** AUM — Assets Under Management; YTD — Year-to-Date; 3Y CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate over 3 years; ER — Expense Ratio.

**Reminder.** A fund is its composition.

**Trap.** Looking at the name, not the composition: a «technology» fund may be half made of the five largest companies already in the portfolio.
