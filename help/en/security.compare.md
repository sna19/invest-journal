# Compare with

**What it is.** What the security belongs to and how it went next to them.

**The idea.** A comparison with the sector, industry and index funds separates the company's own strength from the general current.

**How to use it.**

**References.** For a US share: its **sector** ETF (SPDR: technology — XLK, financials — XLF, healthcare — XLV and others), its **industry** ETF where one exists (semiconductors — SOXX, biotech — IBB, regional banks — KRE, defense — ITA, software — IGV…), and **indices**: Nasdaq 100 (QQQ) for Nasdaq shares, Russell 2000 (IWM) for companies under $5B, S&P 500. For an Israeli share — KSM ETFs on the TASE sector indices (TA-Technology, TA-Finance, TA-RealEstate, TA-Oil&Gas, TA-Construction, TA-Insurance) and on TA-35, TA-90, TA-125. The sector and the industry come from the Yahoo profile.

**The table.** The return of the security and of each reference over 1 month, 3 months, a year and 5 years. Under a reference — by how many pp the security did better (+) or worse (−). If a share rises just like its sector, the rise is the sector's merit, not the company's.

**The tick** next to a reference adds its line to the chart, brought to the same starting point.

The references are always in the daily price load. A reference without history yet is loaded when the card is first opened.

**Reminder.** What does this security go together with?

**Trap.** Comparing with someone else's sector or market.
