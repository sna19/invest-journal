# Income and holders

**What it is.** How much the company pays its shareholders in dividends, and who holds its shares.

**The idea.** A dividend is part of the return that does not depend on the market's mood — but only while it is sustainable. The holders show who else believes in the share: the management with its own money, or large funds.

**How to use it.**

- **Dividend yield** — a year's dividends over the price. 2–4% — usual for mature companies; over 6–7% — either generous or the market doubts the dividend will be kept (the price fell). A high yield after a price fall — check the payout ratio first.
- **Payout ratio** — what part of earnings is paid out. Up to 60% — sustainable, with room; 60–90% — little room for the dividend to grow; over 100% — they pay more than they earn, the dividend is at risk (REITs have their own norm — up to 90%).
- **Insiders** — the share held by the management and founders. 5–20% — their interests match yours; almost 0% — the management does not care about the price.
- **Institutions** — the share held by institutional investors. 60–80% — usual for large US companies; very little — few people study the share (an opportunity or a liquidity risk).

**When it matters.** For dividend positions — at every report: a rising payout ratio is an early sign of a cut.

**Reminder.** A dividend is good only while there is something to pay it from.

**Trap.** Buying the highest dividend yield: often it is a company the market has already punished and that will soon cut its payouts.
