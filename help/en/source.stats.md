# How to read a source's rating

**What it is.** How to read a source's score: the share of hypotheses that worked, its interval and the excess over the benchmark.

**The idea.** A source is worth something only if acting on it beats buying the index — and that shows only over a number of hypotheses.

**How to use it.**

**Share that came true** — how many closed hypotheses of the source beat their benchmark. Next to it — the **95% interval** (Wilson's method): where the true share most likely lies if there were very many hypotheses.

**Example.** 7 out of 10 is not «70%» but «somewhere from 40% to 89%»: ten cases are few, and conclusions are careful. 21 out of 30 is already 52–83%: the interval is narrower, the rating more reliable.

**The order in the list** is by the lower bound of the interval, that is, by what you can count on at least. So a source with 2 out of 2 (lower bound 34%) stands below one with 20 out of 30 (49%): two lucky cases are not proof yet.

**What the rating counts.** Only «Recommendation», «Target price» and «Opinion» closed on time: there the source itself set the direction. **News** is shown apart — you read it. **Closed early** — apart too: the horizon was not waited out. Cancelled ones do not count.

**Excess.** The mean and the median — how much on average the security beat the benchmark. The median depends less on one big case.

**«Without the default term»** removes hypotheses whose horizon was not named in the article and was set to 3 months: that shows whether the rating holds on the source's own horizons.

**Reminder.** How much did this source bring me above the market?

**Trap.** Trusting a source for one or two lucky articles.
