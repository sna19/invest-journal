# Dividends

**What it is.** What each security of the portfolio paid this year and what to expect over the year ahead. Computed by itself: from the exchange's payment history (the ex-date and the amount a share, updated every day) and how many shares you held the day before each ex-date. The sums are before tax, in the portfolio's currency.

**The idea.** A dividend strategy is not «a high percent now» but a reliable and growing flow of money. A share can fall in price and still pay: Israeli banks lose in price in a bad year, but the dividends come — and the total return is better than the chart shows. Without this block that part of the income is out of sight.

**How to use it.**

- **Received in the year** — the payments of what you held. «Recorded» — what you entered by hand as «Dividend» in the portfolio's money (not needed: the count goes by the exchange).
- **A year** — what the current positions bring in 12 months at the past payments; next to it — the dividend yield of all positions.
- **DY** — the payments of 12 months over today's price. **YoC** (yield on cost) — over your purchase price: for a dividend investor it grows over the years even when DY does not.
- **Growth, y** — how many years in a row the yearly dividend grew. 5–10 years and more — the company holds on to its dividend; zero — it pays as it can.
- **✂ cut** — the last payment is well under the one before (or the year under the last one): the main warning of a dividend share — re-read the thesis.
- **Price** and **With div.** — the return since the purchase without the dividends and together with the ones received. The second is the honest result of a dividend share.
- **Next payments** — the ex-dates (announced, or «≈» — by the usual gap) and the expected sum.
- A tap on a row — the year's payments: the date, the amount a share × the shares.

How to choose: the screener → «Dividend» (DY 3%+), then on the card — «Payout ratio» (up to 60–70% — a safety margin) and «Growth, y» here. A very high yield (8% and more) often means the market expects a cut.

**Reminder.** What matters is not the percent now but whether the payment grows and the profit covers it.

**Trap.** Buying the day before the ex-date «for the dividend»: on the ex-date the price falls by about its size, and tax is withheld from the payment too.
