# Calibration

**What it is.** A check of your confidence: if you put 70%, about seven out of ten such hypotheses should come true.

**The idea.** A good decision starts with an honest estimate of the odds; calibration shows how well your percents match reality.

**How to use it.**

**How to read the chart.** Across — the confidence you put, up — the share that came true. The dashed diagonal is perfect calibration. A point below the diagonal — you overrate («I put 70% — 50% comes true»), above — you underrate. The vertical bar is the 95% interval; a pale point — fewer than 10 hypotheses, not to be relied on yet.

**Example.** In the «70–79%» group the mean confidence is 74%, 6 of 12 came true — 50%, interval 25–75%. While the interval is wide the conclusion is careful: «it seems I overrate».

**What counts.** Only hypotheses closed on time where you filled in the confidence, of any signal type. Confidence is optional: hypotheses without it simply stay out of the calibration.

**The summary** at the top — the mean confidence against the overall share that came true.

**Reminder.** Are my 70% really 70%?

**Trap.** Not giving a confidence at all: then it cannot be checked or improved.
