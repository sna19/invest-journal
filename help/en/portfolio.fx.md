# Currencies and the rate

**What it is.** A portfolio lives in its own currency. Money enters it by a deposit or by a **conversion**: it records the shekels given and the dollars received; the deal rate is computed from the two amounts (with the bank fee) and is not used anywhere else. In a dollar portfolio a conversion is its deposit; in a shekel one — a transfer between currencies.

**The idea.** Dollar securities have two returns: from the price and from the rate. Without splitting them it is easy to take luck with the currency for skill at picking.

**How to use it.**

**The balance** is kept by currency, as at the broker (for example, shekels and dollars at the broker). Dollars and dollar securities in a shekel portfolio are converted to shekels at the Bank of Israel representative rate of the date — as the broker shows.

**Growth and rate.** For a security in another currency the result in the portfolio currency splits into the part from the security's price (at the rate of the purchase day) and the part from the rate.

TASE prices are in agorot, amounts in shekels: ₪65 = 6,500 ag.

**Reminder.** Did the security earn this, or the dollar?

**Trap.** Crediting yourself with a rise the rate gave.
