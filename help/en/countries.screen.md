# Countries

**What it is.** About 50 country and regional ETFs listed in the US (EWJ — Japan, EWY — Korea, EPOL — Poland, EIS — Israel, VGK — Europe, EEM — emerging markets…) on one screen: the return over 1, 3, 6 and 12 months, the momentum 12−1 and its rank, the trend, the swings and the year's worst drawdown, the dividend yield, the fund's P/E and its fee.

**The idea.** A country is a bet of its own: its market moves for its own reasons (the economy, the currency, politics), and several countries make a portfolio less dependent on one market. Choosing a country for the next year combines three things: **where the money goes** (momentum, trend), **what is asked for it** (P/E) and **what you would live through** (swings, drawdown).

**How to use it.**

1. **Momentum 12−1** — the year's return less its last month; #1 — the strongest. Research shows strong countries tend to stay strong for a few months more, while the last month rather turns back, so it is left out. The «Country momentum» model stands on it («The model →»): a test on history and a placebo there.
2. **Trend** ↑ — the price over its 200-day average. «Above SMA200 only» drops the falling countries.
3. **P/E** — how dear the country's market is. A low P/E with the trend turning up — a candidate for a return to the mean; a high P/E with strong momentum — the rise is already paid for, more risk.
4. **HV and MDD** — the year's swings and worst drawdown: emerging markets swing twice as hard as developed ones — a smaller position.
5. **DY and fee** — the fund's dividends over 12 months and its yearly fee (0.5% a year shows over a long time).
6. The region — a filter on top; a tap on a header sorts; ☆ picks; «As a set» — the chosen countries as a set: a basket «had I bought them equally a year ago», the «what if» risk against your portfolio, the map of ties (countries of one region often move together).

Returns are by the price without dividends. Funds just added load their history for a few minutes — the rows fill in.

**Reminder.** A country's strength, price and risk — together, not one of them.

**Trap.** Buying the country that rose the most over the year without seeing that it is already dear and that its last month has already turned.
