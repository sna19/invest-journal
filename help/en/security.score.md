# Quant score

**What it is.** One indicator from a security's ratios: a coloured dot next to the ticker (in the card, the portfolio, «Watching» and the model's candidates) and a line under the name in the card. Four factors with equal weights:

- **Quality** — operating and gross margin, ROE, debt to equity;
- **Growth** — revenue and earnings growth year on year;
- **Valuation** — forward P/E, P/S, EV/EBITDA, PEG (the cheaper, the better);
- **Momentum** — the price's rise over 12 months leaving out the last one.

Each metric is the share's place among the shares of **the same sector** (100 — the best; with fewer than 8 shares with data in the sector — among the whole market, marked «*»). A factor is the mean of its metrics, graded A (the best 20%) … F (the worst 20%). The total is the mean of the factors: **green** from 65, **yellow** 35–65, **red** under 35, **grey** — fewer than three factors with data. **The trend rule:** a price under its 200-day average is at most yellow, whatever the other numbers.

**The idea.** This is how the quant ratings of professional services work: metrics compare only within a sector (P/S 8 is average for software and absurd for retail), the factors are shown apart so that the colour does not hide the conflict «an excellent business, but very expensive». Beta is not in it: it does not make a share better or worse, it sets the position's size and stop («Risk», «Protection»).

**How to use it.** The dot is a filter for attention, not an order. Green — worth a closer look; red on a position — a reason to open the thesis. A tap on the line in the card — what each grade is made of. The quant score is kept as a source in the journal: once a month the 10 best green US shares are recorded as hypotheses for 3 months and bought by its virtual portfolio — «Sources» will show whether it beats the market. While the statistics are thin, trust it no more than any new source.

**Reminder.** The colour compresses four questions; the answers are in the line under it.

**Trap.** Buying everything green. The score sees past reports and the price, but knows neither the news, nor the thesis, nor what is already in the portfolio. The ratios are refreshed once a week; for TASE there is no «Valuation» grade — Yahoo's ratios are wrong there.
