# Growth: is the business moving forward

**What it is.** The change of revenue and earnings over the last quarter against the same quarter a year ago.

**The idea.** Over the long run a share's price follows its earnings. Growing earnings drive the price; without growth a share is held up only by dividends or a lower valuation.

**How to use it.**

- **Revenue** — is demand for the product growing. A guide: 5–10% a year — a mature company, 15–25% — a growing one, over 30% — fast (and usually expensive by valuation). Negative two quarters in a row — the business shrinks.
- **Earnings** — is the profit growing. Best when earnings grow faster than revenue: the margin widens. Earnings grow but revenue does not — growth from savings or buybacks, it has an end. Revenue grows but earnings fall — growth is bought dearly.
- Compare the growth with **PEG** and **Forward P/E** in «Valuation»: the market pays for expected growth; if it slows, the valuation shrinks twice.

**When it matters.** After every quarterly report (the dates are in the security's calendar). It is one quarter's number: one quarter is noise, two or three in a row in one direction — a trend.

**Abbreviations.** Rev YoY — Revenue growth Year-over-Year; EPS — Earnings Per Share; EPS YoY — earnings growth year over year.

**Reminder.** Is what I pay for growing?

**Trap.** Comparing a quarter with the previous quarter in a seasonal business (retail, travel): that is why the comparison here is with the same quarter a year ago — and one-off events (selling a division) can distort even that.
