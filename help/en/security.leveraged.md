# Leveraged and inverse ETFs

**What it is.** Funds to play the share's move harder than usual or against it.

**The idea.** Leveraged funds multiply the daily move, not the period's result: over time with swings they melt.

**How to use it.** **Up ×2** — over a day the fund moves about twice the share's change. **Down ×1** — rises when the share falls. First come the funds on the share itself, then on its industry, sector and index. If there are no funds on the share itself, its industry can be played. The leverage is reset every day. Over weeks and months the result drifts from ×N: with strong swings the fund loses even if the share comes back to where it was. So these are tools for short trades, not for long holding. A fund is matched by the share's symbol or the company's name in the fund's name; the leverage and the direction come from the name too. On the right — the **fund's annual fee** (expense ratio): the fund takes it from its own assets, it is already in its price. For leveraged funds it is usually about 1% a year — little for short trades, noticeable for long holding. «by fee» puts the cheapest funds on top. The fee comes from the fund's Yahoo profile: on the first open of the card it is loaded and appears in a minute. Tap a fund's symbol to open its card and chart.

**Reminder.** Is this a trade for days or for months?

**Trap.** Holding a leveraged fund for months like a normal position.
