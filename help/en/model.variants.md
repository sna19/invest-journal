# How the variants differ

**What it is.** All variants look for the same thing — a seasonal rise — but measure it differently.

**The idea.** Each variant answers its own question: did the security rise, did it beat the market, how steadily, was there a reason.

**How to use it.** **A. Price rise** — your original scheme: the share rose in this quarter in K of 10 years. **B. Against the market** — the same, but the rise is counted over the market. In years when everything rose, variant A cannot tell a seasonal share from an ordinary one, and B can. **C. Score** — without a hard threshold: shares are compared by how steadily and how strongly they beat the market. +3% every year counts more than +40% once. **D. With a reason** — like C, but only shares whose seasonality has an explanation: a report, a dividend or a seasonal sector. Which variant is better is not known in advance. The journal will show it after a few quarters.

**Reminder.** Which question does this variant answer?

**Trap.** Going through the variants until one gives the wished answer.
