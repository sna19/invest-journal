# Valuation: what you pay for the business

**What it is.** The share's price divided by the company's results. The higher the number, the more the market pays per unit of earnings, revenue or equity — the more good is already expected of the company.

**The idea.** Expensive does not mean bad, cheap does not mean a bargain. A high valuation is expectations: if the company misses them, the price falls hard. A low one is the market's doubt: sometimes wrong (an opportunity), often right (a «value trap»).

**How to use it.**

- **P/E** — price over the last 12 months' earnings: how many years of today's earnings the share costs. A guide for the US market — about 15–25; under 10 — cheap or troubled, over 30–40 — the market expects fast growth. No value — no earnings (a loss). Compare only within a sector: 8–12 is normal for banks, 30+ for software too.
- **Forward P/E** — the same over the earnings analysts expect next year. Well under the current one — earnings are expected to grow; above — to fall.
- **PEG** — P/E divided by the expected earnings growth in % a year. About 1 — growth justifies the price; under 1 — cheap for that growth; over 2 — expensive even with the growth. Useful for growing companies; does not work for mature or loss-making ones.
- **P/S** — price over revenue. Needed when there are no earnings or they jump (young and growing companies). A guide: 1–3 — usual, over 10 — very high expectations. Read it with the margin: P/S 5 at a 30% margin and at a 3% margin are very different prices.
- **P/B** — price over book value (assets less debts). The main ratio for banks and insurers: under 1 — sold below its assets (or the assets are doubted). Almost useless for software and brands: their value is not on the balance sheet.
- **EV/EBITDA** — the company's value with its debt over operating profit before depreciation. Fairer than P/E when companies carry different debt. A guide: 6–12 — usual, under 6 — cheap or the cycle at its top, over 15–20 — expensive. Not used for banks.

**When it matters.** Before a purchase — always; when reviewing a position — after a big rise: has the valuation run ahead of the growth? Compare with the sector's neighbours in «Compare with» and with the ratios' snapshots in the diary.

**Abbreviations.** P/E — Price-to-Earnings; Fwd P/E — Forward P/E; P/S — Price-to-Sales; P/B — Price-to-Book; EV — Enterprise Value; EBITDA — Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation & Amortization; PEG — Price/Earnings-to-Growth.

**Reminder.** Valuation asks «how much is already in the price», not «is it a good company».

**Trap.** A low P/E of a cyclical company (steel, oil, chips) at the top of the cycle: earnings are high for a while, and a year later the P/E is «suddenly» high because the earnings fell.
