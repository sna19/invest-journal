# How many would pass by chance

**What it is.** Among thousands of shares there are always some that rose in one quarter almost every year, by plain chance, like a run of heads in coin tosses.

**The idea.** Among thousands of securities coincidences are certain. The model makes sense only if it finds clearly more than chance does.

**How to use it.**

**The estimate.** If the share of rising quarters in the universe is p, a share with no seasonality passes the «K of 10» filter with the probability P(at least K successes of 10 at probability p). Multiplied by the universe size N, this gives how many shares would pass by chance alone.

**Example.** N = 2,000, p = 55%, K = 9: about 2,000 × 0.023 ≈ 46 shares would pass by chance. If 50 passed, what was found is almost entirely luck. If 200 did, there seems to be seasonality, but even then some candidates are random.

This is an approximate formula: it treats the years as independent. The model screen now shows the placebo check instead — it is more exact.

**Reminder.** More than chance would find?

**Trap.** Being glad of a long list of candidates without comparing it with the chance number.
