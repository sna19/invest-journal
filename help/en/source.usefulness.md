# What a source's rating measures

**What it is.** The app checks not whether a source is **truthful**, but whether **acting after reading it pays**.

**The idea.** A truthful source can be useless if its news is already in the price; what matters is the gain of acting, not accuracy.

**How to use it.** An outlet can write accurately and honestly, yet by the time of publication the market has already priced the news in — and buying after reading gives nothing. The reverse happens too: controversial columns after which the security steadily beats the market. For the decision «should I buy on this source» the second is what matters.

**How it is computed.** Each checkable claim is recorded on the day you read it; at the horizon the security is compared with the benchmark. The share of hypotheses that came true is shown with a confidence interval and only after 10 closed — before that there is no conclusion.

**What is not in the rating.** News (its direction is your reading) — a separate row. Closed early — apart too.

**For the rating to be honest,** record all checkable claims of an article, not only the ones you liked, and do not change the horizon to fit your expectation.

**Reminder.** Is it profitable to act on this source?

**Trap.** Mixing up «the source is right» with «one can make money on it».
